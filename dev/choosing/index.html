ML-KEM or X-Wing

ML-KEM is the NIST standard. X-Wing combines it with X25519, so a session stays confidential unless both are broken: a classical attacker must break X25519 and a quantum one ML-KEM-768. X-Wing is an Internet-Draft, not yet an RFC; its wire format may still change.

ML-DSA or Falcon

ML-DSA is the NIST standard and the simpler scheme to implement safely. Falcon gives signatures about a fifth the size, at the cost of a more delicate signer (Gaussian sampling over floating point, emulated in integers here). FN-DSA, NIST's standard based on Falcon, is not yet published; this package implements the Falcon round-3 specification, with options for the changes NIST has announced (Security).

SLH-DSA

ML-DSA, ML-KEM and Falcon all rest on lattice problems; SLH-DSA rests only on the security of its hash function, the most conservative assumption available. The price is size and signing time: signatures of 8–50 KB, and signing that takes 0.4–0.8 s in the small (s) sets or 20–90 ms in the fast (f) sets. It suits signatures made rarely and kept long — firmware, root certificates, archives — and pairs with ML-DSA where a second, independent assumption is wanted.

The SHA2 and SHAKE sets are equally secure; choose the hash your platform already relies on. s sets give the smaller signature and f sets the faster signer. Keys are tiny either way (public key 32–64 bytes).

Pure or pre-hash signing

MLDSA65.sign signs the message itself. MLDSA65.sign_prehash (HashML-DSA) signs a hash of it, for messages too large to hold in memory or when the signer only sees a digest. The two produce different signatures, and a verifier must use the matching function.

Context strings

ML-DSA binds an optional context (up to 255 bytes) into the signature: a signature made with context = b"app-v1" verifies only with the same context. Use one per protocol or purpose to keep signatures from being replayed across them.
